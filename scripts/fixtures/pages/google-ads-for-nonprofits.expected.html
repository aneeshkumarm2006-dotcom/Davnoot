Why nonprofit campaigns are different

Nonprofit advertising has a
different goal.

Success here is a donation processed, a volunteer application submitted, a seat filled at an event, or somebody who needs a service reaching the program that provides it. None of those is a sale. An account built to chase sales will not measure any of them.

The objective decides the rest of the account. The campaign structure, the keywords, the landing pages, and the conversion tracking all have to support the action the organization wants people to take. When one of those four points somewhere else, the campaign spends money on the wrong thing and never looks broken in the account.

Most nonprofit accounts we open are broken in the same dull way. Nobody wrote the objective down, so the keywords came from the cause instead of the action, every advertisement points at the homepage, and the reporting counts clicks because nothing else was ever set up to count.

This page covers the nonprofit version of that account. The platform detail underneath it (bidding, match types, ad formats, reporting) lives on our Google Ads management page.

What we manage

Six parts of a nonprofit
account, managed together.

These six settings only make sense in relation to each other, so we manage them as one account rather than as a monthly checklist.

01

Campaign structure by objective

A nonprofit usually has several programs or campaigns live at once. We give donations, volunteer opportunities, events, and programs and services a campaign each, so the budget can be set per objective and it stays clear which one is producing results.

02

Nonprofit keyword research

Nonprofit campaigns attract readers as well as people ready to act. We sort candidate keywords by search intent, cause and program, location, search volume, competition, relevance, and the conversion action expected at the end of the click.

03

Google Ad Grants guidance

Some organizations qualify for the Google Ad Grants program. We check Google's current eligibility and program requirements with you first, then manage the account the same way whether the budget is granted or your own.

04

A landing page per campaign

The page has to answer the reason for the click. Donation, volunteer, event, and program campaigns each get their own page and their own next step instead of sharing the homepage.

05

Negative keywords and search terms

Nonprofit keywords produce a lot of searches that have nothing to do with the objective. We read the search-term report and keep the negative keyword list current, so the campaign stays in front of people who can actually act.

06

Tracking for donations and applications

We set up donations, volunteer applications, registrations, form submissions, program enquiries, and newsletter signups as tracked conversions, so the decisions get made against contributions instead of clicks.

Keyword research

Keyword research for
nonprofit campaigns.

Nonprofit campaigns attract a mixture of informational and action-oriented searches, and the two do not belong in the same ad group. Somebody searching for “how to help homeless people” has a different intent from somebody searching for “homeless shelter volunteer opportunities.” One is reading. The other is ready to sign up.

So every candidate keyword gets graded before it goes into a campaign. This is what we grade it on.

We are not trying to cover every search connected to the cause. The list that survives is much shorter than the one a keyword tool hands back, and every keyword left on it matches something the nonprofit actually offers.

Campaign structure

One nonprofit goal,
one campaign.

Several programs and campaigns usually run at the same time. Keeping them apart makes the budget easier to control and shows which one is producing results, instead of one blended number nobody can act on.

Each campaign then uses the keywords and the landing page that match its specific goal. That is the whole benefit of the split: a donor search and a volunteer search stop competing for the same budget, and neither one lands on a page written for the other.

01

Donation campaigns

People looking to donate or to support a specific cause. The keywords, the advertisement, and the page all point at one thing: completing a contribution.

02

Volunteer opportunities

People searching for volunteer opportunities or for ways to get involved, usually with a cause or a neighbourhood already attached to the search.

03

Fundraising appeals

A specific fundraising campaign with its own timeline and its own target, kept out of the general donations campaign so its budget and its results stay readable.

04

Events and registrations

People looking for fundraising events, community events, or nonprofit activities, sent to the event details and the registration information rather than to a general page.

06

Awareness of the cause

Increasing awareness of an organization or a cause, measured on softer actions such as newsletter signups rather than on donations it was never structured to produce.

Google Ad Grants

Google Ad Grants still
needs managing.

Some nonprofits qualify for Google Ad Grants, which gives them advertising support through Google Ads.

Eligibility and program requirements apply, and they are Google's to change. Check the current requirements directly with Google before building a strategy around Ad Grants, rather than around what the program allowed last year.

A granted budget behaves like any other budget. Relevant keywords, useful landing pages, conversion tracking, and ongoing optimization are what turn it into something. A grant that gets spent is not the same as a grant that brought in donations.

Where the click lands

The homepage is not a
donation page.

The landing page has to answer the reason for the click. Send a donation click to a page that explains the cause and takes the donation. Send a volunteer click to the opportunity and the next steps. Send an event click to the details and the registration information.

Sending every campaign to the organization's homepage makes it harder for visitors to find what they were looking for. The click has already been paid for by then, which is why the choice of landing page usually moves cost per donation more than a bid change does.

One page per objective is the working rule. If two campaigns share a landing page, at least one of them is asking a visitor to read past the thing they did not come for.

Donations and volunteers

Campaigns for donors,
campaigns for volunteers.

On a donation campaign the path from the advertisement to the donation should be short. The page explains what the organization does, what the donation supports, how contributions are used, which donation options exist, and how to complete it.

We track the donation itself. Otherwise the report shows visits to the donate page and calls them results.

Volunteer campaigns answer a different search. They reach people looking for opportunities near them or for a cause they already care about: volunteer opportunities, volunteer programs, community volunteering, cause-specific volunteering, and local volunteer opportunities.

The volunteer landing page has four jobs. Explain who the organization is, what volunteers will actually do, where the opportunities are available, and how to apply. A page that stops after the first two collects sympathy rather than applications.

Track more than clicks

is only the beginning.

A click tells you somebody was interested enough to arrive. It does not tell you whether the organization got a donation, a volunteer, or an enquiry out of it, and those are the only numbers a board can act on.

Where nonprofit campaigns struggle

Most nonprofit campaigns struggle
before the budget does.

The advertising objective was never defined. That is the first problem, and the rest follow from it: keywords that are too broad, every visitor sent to the homepage, no negative keywords, weak conversion tracking, several nonprofit goals mixed into one campaign, clicks treated as the result, and search terms nobody reads.

Nonprofit keywords are unusually good at attracting the wrong search. One campaign pulls in people looking for jobs, students working on school assignments, people after general information, people after free resources, people looking for an organization with a similar name, and people wanting a service the nonprofit does not provide.

We strip that traffic out with search-term reviews and negative keywords. Fixing it makes the campaign easier to manage on the budget it already has.

Local targeting

Google Ads for nonprofits
in Montreal.

For a Montreal nonprofit we target the searches happening nearby, in the communities the organization actually serves. Somebody searching for a shelter, a food bank, or a family program wants one near them.

Location targeting matters most for local programs, volunteer opportunities, community services, and events. It is also the setting that stops a limited budget from paying for clicks in another province.

An organization working across several locations gets a campaign per service area, so each area's donations and volunteer applications can be read on their own instead of as one blended total.

Serving the communities the organization works with also means reaching them in the language they search in. In Montreal that is a targeting decision as much as a writing one, because a French donation search and an English donation search are two different queries, with their own keywords, their own landing page, and their own cost.

FAQ

Straight answers on
nonprofit Google Ads.

Can nonprofits use Google Ads?+
Yes. Nonprofits can run Google Ads campaigns, and some also qualify for Google's Ad Grants program. Check the current eligibility and program requirements directly with Google.
What can nonprofits advertise on Google?+
Donations, volunteer opportunities, events, programs, services, and awareness of the cause. Each one belongs in its own campaign.
What keywords should nonprofits use?+
Keywords that match the organization's actual cause, programs, location, and campaign objective. Intent matters more than volume: a short list of searches from people ready to act beats a long list of people reading.
Should nonprofits send Google Ads traffic to their homepage?+
No. Send each campaign to a page built for its objective, so the visitor lands on the thing they clicked for instead of hunting for it.
How should nonprofits measure Google Ads?+
On the action the campaign was built to produce. Donations, volunteer applications, registrations, enquiries, and any other action that matters get tracked as conversions. Clicks on their own do not tell the organization anything it can act on.
