Why it works

Google Ads for
HVAC companies.

Nobody searches for heating and cooling out of curiosity. They search when the AC has stopped, when the furnace starts making a noise, when the system is old enough to replace. The search itself names the service and the urgency, and most of the time the area too.

That makes HVAC one of the clearer cases for paid search. We build the campaign around those searches and around the cities and service areas the company covers, so the budget only meets people who can actually be sent a technician.

This page covers the HVAC side of that work: the services, the search terms, the local fence, and what counts as a lead. How an account gets managed week to week, in any industry, is on our PPC agency Montreal page.

HVAC Google Ads campaigns

HVAC service.

We separate the campaigns by service so the ad matches what the customer is looking for. Someone typing "furnace repair" and someone pricing a full HVAC replacement are at different points, worth different money, and should not share a budget.

Split this way, the budget stays controllable and it is obvious which services generate enquiries. We can also bid an emergency HVAC search differently from a planned installation, because it is a different kind of customer.

Local HVAC search advertising

HVAC ads that stop
at your service area.

Someone searching for an HVAC contractor needs a company that serves their address. A firm two hours away is not a competitor for that job. It is just a click you both paid for.

So we fence the targeting to specific cities and service areas rather than sending ads to places the company does not serve. For a heating and cooling business with a fixed radius, that fence is often the single largest saving in the account.

When a company covers several areas, we separate them in the account. Cost per click and cost per HVAC lead are rarely the same across a metro region, and one blended average hides the area that is quietly funding the rest.

Emergency and seasonal HVAC demand

Heating and cooling
do not peak together.

An HVAC account has more than one season in it. AC repair searches arrive with the first stretch of heat and furnace repair searches arrive with the first cold snap, so a budget set once and left alone is the wrong budget for most of the year.

Emergency HVAC searches behave differently again. The decision takes minutes, the job goes to whoever answers, and nobody is collecting quotes. We keep those searches in their own campaign so they can be bid and scheduled on their own terms.

Planned work runs on a longer clock. HVAC replacement, air conditioning installation and commercial HVAC contracts all get researched before anyone calls, so we fund them steadily rather than switching them off whenever the repair campaigns get busy.

Generate HVAC leads

An HVAC lead is a call,
not a click.

A conversion in an HVAC account is a phone call, a service request, a quote request, an appointment booking or a contact form. Those five are not worth the same money, and an account counting clicks cannot tell them apart.

Call and form tracking is what separates them. With it, you can see which campaigns and which services are producing leads. Without it, an HVAC account gets judged on clicks, and clicks are the easiest number in the account to move for no reason.

Most heating and cooling companies book the majority of their work by phone, so we track calls as their own conversion rather than folding them into a single count of form fills. A busy phone and an empty inbox is a working campaign, not a broken one.

HVAC landing pages

Furnace repair searches deserve
a furnace repair page.

Send a furnace repair search to a general HVAC homepage and the customer has to go find the service again, three clicks down. The ad made a promise; the page either keeps it or hands the work back to the customer.

A dedicated service page keeps the ad and the landing page pointed at the same search, and gives the customer a clearer next step. It also makes the account easier to read, because each service can be measured against the page it sends traffic to.

FAQ

Straight answers on
HVAC Google Ads.

Can Google Ads help HVAC companies generate leads?+
Yes. Google Ads puts an HVAC company in front of people already searching for repair, installation, maintenance, replacement and other heating and cooling services. Whether those clicks turn into leads depends on the targeting, the landing page and the tracking behind them.
Should HVAC Google Ads target specific locations?+
Yes. HVAC work is location-specific, so campaigns should target only the areas the company serves. A click from outside the service area cannot become a booked job, and it costs the same as one that could.
What HVAC keywords should be targeted?+
Keywords should match the services you sell: AC repair, furnace repair, HVAC installation, heating services and the other searches that arrive with a job attached. Terms about jobs, training, DIY repairs, manuals, parts and free information belong on the negative list instead.
How do you measure HVAC Google Ads?+
We measure calls, service enquiries, quote requests, bookings, cost per lead and conversion rate. Click-through rate, cost per click and cost per acquisition fill in the rest of the picture, and all of them get read together rather than one at a time.
