for dentists.

Google Ads can be an effective way for dental practices to reach people searching for dental services in their area. The opportunity is not just to get more website visits, but to focus advertising on the searches that are relevant to the treatments your practice actually offers.

A practice may want to promote general dentistry, dental implants, Invisalign, emergency dentistry, or cosmetic treatments. Each one has different search intent, different competition, and a different cost.

Google Ads for dental practices

Build the campaign around
the treatment, not the clinic.

A dental Google Ads campaign should be built around the services and locations that matter to the practice. Instead of using one broad campaign for every service, campaigns can be organized around specific areas.

Splitting them this way makes it easier to match keywords, advertisements, landing pages, and budget to each service — and to see which treatments are actually producing appointment requests rather than clicks.

Target the right dental searches

The search term tells you
what they came for.

"Dentist near me" usually means someone looking for a local practice. "Dental implants" or "Invisalign dentist" indicates interest in a specific treatment. Those are different campaigns, with different budgets and different pages behind them.

Location targeting matters just as much. A dental practice generally wants to reach people who can realistically visit the clinic, rather than paying for clicks from areas outside its service area.

01
Service

Which treatment is the search about?

A general dentistry search and an implant search behave nothing alike. Keeping them in one ad group means the cheaper clicks quietly spend the budget the expensive treatment needed.

02
Intent and competition

How ready is this person to book?

Someone comparing aligner brands is at a different stage from someone with a broken tooth at 8pm. Intent, competition, and cost per click all move together.

03
Location

Can this person actually visit the clinic?

A practice draws patients from a realistic radius. Advertising well outside it produces clicks from people who were never going to sit in the chair.

04
Value

What is this patient worth?

Budget follows the potential value of the service. What is expensive for a whitening enquiry can be inexpensive for a full implant case.

Dental Google Ads campaign management

More than keywords
and ad copy.

A well-managed campaign involves more than choosing keywords and writing advertisements. These are the six parts we manage together.

01

Keyword research

Identify the relevant searches for the dental services and locations being promoted, and separate the treatment searches from the general ones.

02

Campaign structure

Organize campaigns and ad groups around services and search intent, so each treatment has its own keywords, budget, and reporting line.

03

Ad copy

Write advertisements that are relevant to the service being searched for and communicate the next step clearly, whether that is a call or an appointment request.

04

Negative keywords

Remove the irrelevant searches that use up advertising budget without producing useful enquiries, and keep adding to that list as new ones appear.

05

Location targeting

Focus campaigns on the areas where the practice actually serves patients, with exclusions for the places a click can never turn into a visit.

06

Ongoing optimization

Review search terms, keywords, ads, conversions, and spending to identify what needs to be adjusted, rather than leaving the account to run on its original settings.

Match ads with the right page

An implant search should not land
on the homepage.

A person searching for dental implants should not have to search through a general homepage to find information about implants. The more relevant path is short: implant search, implant ad, implant page, appointment enquiry.

The same approach applies to Invisalign, emergency dentistry, cosmetic dentistry, and any other major service. The landing page should give useful information about the treatment and make it easy to contact the practice or request an appointment.

If those treatment pages don't exist yet, that is a website problem before it is an advertising one, and it is worth solving alongside search engine optimization so the same pages earn organic visits too.

Track calls, enquiries and appointments

Clicks are only one part
of the performance.

For a dental practice, most of the valuable actions happen off the page or on the phone. Conversion tracking is what tells you which campaigns and keywords are generating meaningful enquiries rather than simply website traffic.

It also makes cost per lead legible. Once you know what an implant enquiry costs and what a whitening enquiry costs, deciding where the budget should go stops being a matter of opinion.

How much do Google Ads for dentists cost

There is no fixed price
for a dental campaign.

Advertising costs vary depending on the location, competition, the services being promoted, keyword demand, and campaign budget. Advertising for a competitive treatment such as dental implants can cost very differently from advertising for general dentistry.

The right budget should therefore be based on the services you want to promote, the number of new patient enquiries you want to generate, and the value of acquiring a new patient. Anyone quoting a monthly figure before asking those three things is guessing.

FAQ

Straight answers on
dental Google Ads.

Are Google Ads effective for dentists?+
Google Ads can help dental practices reach people actively searching for dental services. Results depend on keyword targeting, competition, location, budget, landing pages, and conversion tracking, so the honest answer for any particular practice comes from looking at those first.
What keywords should dentists use for Google Ads?+
Keywords should be based on the services and locations the practice wants to promote. Examples include dentist near me, dental implants, Invisalign dentist, emergency dentist, and cosmetic dentist. Which of those are worth funding depends on what the practice offers.
Should dentists use separate campaigns for different services?+
Separating major services makes it easier to manage keywords, advertisements, budgets, and landing pages, and to see which treatments are producing enquiries. The right structure depends on the size of the practice and its advertising goals.
How can dentists reduce wasted Google Ads spend?+
Use relevant keywords, appropriate location targeting, negative keywords, and relevant landing pages. Reviewing search terms regularly is what identifies where budget is being spent on searches that were never going to become appointments.
Should Google Ads for dentists go to the homepage?+
Not necessarily. A specific search should ideally lead to a relevant page. Someone searching for dental implants should be able to find detailed implant information without having to navigate through the entire website first.
Do you run dental campaigns in French?+
Yes. In Quebec a practice usually needs both, and the two audiences search with different words. Running them as separate campaigns rather than one translated campaign keeps the reporting readable and the budgets independent.
