Platforms we work with

The right platform
is the boring one.

Catalogue size, order volume, how your ops team actually works, and what you plan to sell in three years. Those decide the platform. We don't recommend the same one to everyone, and if your current store is fine, we will tell you that instead of quoting a migration.

Shopify
Most stores, most of the time

Fast to launch, cheap to run, and the ecosystem covers almost every edge case. The default unless something specific rules it out.

Shopify Plus
High volume, complex ops

Worth it for checkout control, multi-storefront, B2B, and the automation that stops your team doing the same task by hand every morning.

WooCommerce
Content-led catalogues

When the store lives inside a serious content site, or you need ownership and control of the stack that hosted platforms won't give you.

Magento
Large, established catalogues

Usually inherited rather than chosen. We maintain and optimize Magento stores, and we're straight with you about when moving off it pays for itself.

Headless
Custom front ends

A decoupled storefront when the experience genuinely needs it. A real cost in complexity, so it needs a real reason beyond speed scores.

Custom builds
Models nothing off-the-shelf fits

Subscription, rental, configurators, marketplace mechanics. Built by the same team that writes our custom software.

Is your store ready to scale

If any of these sound
familiar, we can help.

Growth rarely comes from changing one thing. It usually comes from fixing several that quietly depend on each other. These are the signals that usually mean more than one is broken.

Most stores that plateau are not failing at any single thing. Traffic is fine, the ads work on paper, the store looks good. But the parts were built by different people at different times against different goals, and nobody owns the number they add up to.

The first thing we do is find which one is actually capping the others. Sometimes it's the checkout. Sometimes it's a product feed nobody has opened in a year. It is almost never the thing you were about to spend the budget on.

FAQ

Straight answers
on ecommerce.

What does an ecommerce agency do?+
An ecommerce agency helps a business grow online sales across the whole path a shopper takes: building and maintaining the store, earning organic search traffic, running paid advertising, improving conversion, and keeping customers through email and retention. The useful ones connect those pieces rather than selling them as separate projects, because the constraint on revenue is usually the handoff between them.
Do you only work with Shopify stores?+
No. We work extensively with Shopify and Shopify Plus, and we also support WooCommerce, Magento, headless storefronts, and fully custom builds. The platform should follow your catalogue, order volume, and how your operations team actually works, so we recommend based on those rather than defaulting everyone to the same answer.
Can you improve an existing ecommerce website?+
Yes, and it's most of what we do. Many projects are redesigns, SEO and performance work, and conversion-focused updates to a store that already sells, rather than a rebuild from scratch. Rebuilding throws away the pages and rankings you already paid for, so we only recommend it when the platform or the codebase is genuinely the thing holding revenue back.
Do I need SEO if I'm already running paid ads?+
They solve different problems. Paid buys visibility immediately and stops the day you stop paying; SEO compounds slowly and keeps working. Stores that rely only on paid tend to watch their cost per acquisition rise every year with no floor under it. Running both means the paid budget is a growth lever rather than the only thing keeping revenue alive.
How long does ecommerce SEO take?+
Technical fixes can move things within weeks; category and product rankings usually take three to six months to show meaningful movement, and competitive categories take longer. The honest answer is that it depends on your starting technical health, your catalogue size, and how established the sites ahead of you are. Anyone quoting a fixed timeline before looking at your store is guessing.
Can Davnoot manage both development and marketing?+
Yes, and it's the reason most clients come to us. One team covers store development, SEO, Google Ads, Meta advertising, email and retention, so the roadmap stays connected and nobody is waiting on a vendor who doesn't answer to the same goal. You can also engage us for a single discipline if that's the gap you actually have.
