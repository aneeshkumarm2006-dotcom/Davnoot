Free marketing tools.

Every tool here runs entirely inside your own browser. Nothing you type or paste is uploaded to a server, stored in a database, or logged anywhere, because there is no server behind these pages to send it to. None of them ask for an email address before showing you a result.

We build them because we needed them on client work first. When an internal script turns out to be useful enough to deserve a real interface, it ends up on this page instead of in a folder on somebody's laptop.

Content Analyzer
Paste a draft, set a focus keyword, and get a weighted score out of 100 with the specific changes that would raise it: word count, heading structure, where the keyword actually appears, sentence and paragraph length, internal and external link balance, and how much of your imagery is missing alt text. What we are building next.

These are the ones being worked on. They are listed as plain text on purpose — nothing gets a link until it is finished, because a page that loads and does nothing is worse than a page that does not exist yet. No dates, either. They ship when they are good.

Schema markup generator
Fill in a short form, get valid JSON-LD for an article, a product, a local business, or an FAQ block, with the fields Google actually reads flagged separately from the ones it ignores.
Search ad preview and length checker
Headlines and descriptions measured against the real character limits, with a live preview of how the ad truncates on a phone rather than on a desktop mockup nobody sees.
Social ad copy previewer
Primary text, headline, and description shown at the point where the feed cuts them off, across the placements where the crop is different.
Subject line tester
Subject line and preheader checked for length, clipping in the major inboxes, emoji rendering, and the spam-trigger phrasing that quietly costs you deliverability.
llms.txt generator
A correctly formatted llms.txt file for your site, so assistants that read one can find the pages you want quoted instead of guessing from a sitemap.
Product page checker
The store-specific checks a general page audit misses: duplicate variant URLs, thin descriptions repeated across a catalogue, missing product markup, and collection pages competing with each other.
The honest answer

Why we give these away.

Three reasons, and none of them are generous.

The first is that they cost us almost nothing to run. A tool with no server, no database, and no accounts is just a file. Once it is written, serving it to ten thousand people a month costs the same as serving it to nobody, and there is no support queue attached to it. Charging for something that cheap would be more work than giving it away.

The second is that this is how a lot of people find us. Someone searching for free SEO tools usually has the same problem our clients have, only earlier and with a smaller budget. We would rather be useful at that point than pay to interrupt the same person six months later. If a tool helps and you never speak to us, that is a fine outcome. If it helps and you eventually want the work done for you, you already know how we think.

The third is the one that matters most to us. A tool that actually works is a better sample of our judgment than a case study. A case study is our account of something you cannot verify. A tool you can break in thirty seconds, and if the scoring is soft or the advice is generic, you will know immediately. Publishing one is a standing invitation to check whether we know what we are talking about.

If you want the reasoning behind the checks rather than the checks themselves, the free training and the blog go considerably deeper. If you would rather hand the whole thing over, our services and our pricing are both public.

FAQ

Free tools
FAQs.

Are these free marketing tools really free?+
Yes. No account, no trial, no credit card, no usage limit, and no paid tier waiting behind a locked result. There is nothing to upgrade to, because there is no billing attached to any of it.
Do you store or read what I paste in?+
No, and not as a policy promise — as a technical fact. All the analysis happens in JavaScript inside your own browser. Your text is never sent anywhere, so there is nowhere for us to store it and nothing for us to read.
Do I need to give you an email address?+
No. There is no gate, no unlock step, and no results-by-email screen. Open the page, use the tool, close the tab. We would rather you remember the tool than resent the form.
Which one should I start with?+
Right now there is one: the Content Analyzer. Paste a draft you are about to publish and see what it flags before it goes live rather than after.
Will these replace paid software?+
No, and we would not claim otherwise. Anything that needs live search data, crawl history, or competitor indexes needs a paid platform behind it. These tools cover the parts that can be worked out from the page in front of you, which is a surprisingly large share of the day-to-day.
How often do you add new ones?+
Whenever something we built for our own work is worth handing over. We deliberately do not commit to a schedule, because a release date is the fastest way to ship a tool that is not ready. The in-progress list above is the real roadmap.
