Free marketing tools.

Two things live here, and they are deliberately different. The analyzer is software: it runs entirely inside your own browser, nothing you paste is uploaded, and no address is asked for before you see the result. The site audit is the opposite — a person on our team reads your site by hand and emails you what they find, so it needs a way to reach you and a few days to do properly.

We build the software because we needed it on client work first. When an internal script turns out to be useful enough to deserve a real interface, it ends up on this page instead of in a folder on somebody's laptop.

Two are live. One answers in a second because your own machine does the work; the other takes three days because a person does. The list grows as we finish the next ones, and everything that lands here stays free.

Content Analyzer
Paste a draft, set a focus keyword, and get a weighted score out of 100 with the specific changes that would raise it: word count, heading structure, where the keyword actually appears, sentence and paragraph length, internal and external link balance, and how much of your imagery is missing alt text. It reads HTML and Markdown, so a pasted CMS export works as well as plain prose.
Free · Instant · Runs in your browser
Free Site Audit
Tell us the site and someone here goes through it by hand: what you rank for against what you should, whether Google can read the whole thing, whether the key pages answer the question a buyer arrived with, where ad budget is leaking, whether your tracking is telling the truth, and whether AI answers mention you at all. You get the findings in an email, in the order we would fix them.
Free · Three business days · Done by a person

The honest answer

Why we give these away.

Three reasons, and none of them are generous.

The first is that the software costs us almost nothing to run. A tool with no server, no database and no accounts is just a file: once it is written, serving it to ten thousand people a month costs the same as serving it to nobody, and there is no support queue attached. Charging for something that cheap would be more work than giving it away. The audit is the exception — that one costs us hours of a real person, and we give it away for the reason below.

The second is that this is how a lot of people find us. Someone searching for free SEO tools usually has the same problem our clients have, only earlier and with a smaller budget. We would rather be useful at that point than pay to interrupt the same person six months later. If a tool helps and you never speak to us, that is a fine outcome. If it helps and you eventually want the work done for you, you already know how we think.

The third is the one that matters most to us. A tool that actually works is a better sample of our judgment than a case study. A case study is our account of something you cannot verify. A tool you can break in thirty seconds, and if the scoring is soft or the advice is generic, you will know immediately. Publishing one is a standing invitation to check whether we know what we are talking about.

If you want the reasoning behind the checks rather than the checks themselves, the free training and the blog go considerably deeper. If you would rather hand the whole thing over, our services and our pricing are both public.

FAQs.

Are these free marketing tools really free?+
Yes. No account, no trial, no credit card, no usage limit, and no paid tier waiting behind a locked result. There is nothing to upgrade to, because there is no billing attached to any of it.
Do you store or read what I paste in?+
Not in the analyzer, and not as a policy promise — as a technical fact. All of that analysis happens in JavaScript inside your own browser, so there is nowhere for us to store your text and nothing for us to read. The site audit is the other way round by necessity: you are asking a person to read your site, so we keep your request and your address until we have answered it.
Do I need to give you an email address?+
Not to use the analyzer. There is no gate, no unlock step and no results-by-email screen — open the page, use the tool, close the tab. The audit obviously needs one, because the whole deliverable is an email from a person. And if you want a human to read a draft after the tool has scored it, there is an optional field for that under the result. None of it is required to see a score.
Which one should I start with?+
If you have a page in front of you, the Content Analyzer — paste a draft you are about to publish and see what it flags before it goes live rather than after. If you do not know where the problem is, ask for the free site audit and let somebody else find it.
Will these replace paid software?+
No, and we would not claim otherwise. Anything that needs live search data, crawl history, or competitor indexes needs a paid platform behind it. These tools cover the parts that can be worked out from the page in front of you, which is a surprisingly large share of the day-to-day.
How often do you add new ones?+
Whenever something we built for our own work is worth handing over. We deliberately do not commit to a schedule, because a release date is the fastest way to ship a tool that is not ready. The in-progress list above is the real roadmap.
