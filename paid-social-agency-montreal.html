Paid social campaign management

The objective comes
before the first ad.

We manage paid social campaigns around the audience, offer, budget, and objective of the business. A campaign can be structured around any number of goals, and the right structure is the one that reflects what the business is trying to achieve rather than the one that maximizes impressions or clicks.

Those goals are not interchangeable. A campaign built to fill a form and a campaign built to sell a product want different audiences, different creative, and different budgets, and they stop being comparable the moment they are put in the same report.

So the first decision is which of these the campaign is for, and the second is what would count as it working. Both are business questions, and both are easier to answer before the account is built than after three months of spend. A campaign with no agreed definition of success will always look like it is doing something.

Facebook and Instagram advertising

One campaign to introduce,
another to follow up.

Facebook and Instagram reach both new and existing audiences. They sit in the same ad account and share the same audiences and the same creative library, so they are worth planning together rather than treating as two separate media buys.

We create campaigns around different audience groups and objectives, with ads adapted to where people are in the buying process. Someone who has never heard of the business needs the offer explained. Someone who read a service page last week does not.

One campaign introduces a service to a new audience. Another reaches people who have already visited the website or interacted with the business. Splitting them is also what makes the reporting readable, because the two will never produce the same cost per result.

Audience targeting

Who sees the ad
is a decision.

Paid social gives a business several ways to define an audience. Targeting gets built from location, demographics, interests, online behaviour, website visitors, existing customers, previous engagement, and similar or lookalike audiences where they are available.

For Montreal businesses, location targeting keeps the spend on the areas the business actually serves. A service business with a two-borough radius and a store shipping across the province are not the same campaign, even when the ad is identical.

Which signals to combine is a campaign-by-campaign decision. A prospecting audience and a retargeting audience are defined in opposite directions, and the audience that worked for one offer is not automatically the audience for the next one.

Paid social ads and creative testing

The audience is only
half the campaign.

The creative has to give people a reason to stop, understand the offer, and take the next step. A well-defined audience shown a forgettable ad is still a forgettable ad, and the platform will keep delivering it for as long as the budget holds out.

So we test different combinations of images and video, ad copy, headlines, offers, and calls to action. We change one part at a time against the same audience, because a test that moves three things at once gives you a result without giving you the reason for it.

Campaign data shows which messages and creative formats are producing better results, instead of assuming one advertisement works for everyone. The same data tells you when a creative has run long enough and needs replacing.

Retargeting campaigns

Not everyone buys
on the first visit.

A visitor who leaves without enquiring is rarely an uninterested visitor. Most of them were just not ready to buy that day. Retargeting reaches people who have already interacted with a business, such as website visitors or previous ad engagers, with an ad written on the assumption that they already know who you are.

These campaigns bring visitors back to a product page, follow up with potential leads, promote an offer, remind visitors about a service, and move people further through the buying process. Each of those wants its own message rather than the prospecting ad shown a second time.

The audience and the retargeting period should be appropriate to the business and the type of purchase being considered. A repeat purchase somebody decides on in a minute and a service that takes two months to choose do not deserve the same window.

Paid social for lead generation

More forms is not
the same as more customers.

For service businesses, paid social generates enquiries through landing pages or lead forms. A campaign targets people in Montreal who fit the business's customer profile and points them at one specific service or offer.

Then track those leads through the process, not just to the form. That is what shows the business which campaigns are producing useful enquiries, and a cheap lead that never becomes work is not actually cheap.

That tracking is also what makes the campaign manageable. Once enquiries can be traced back to the audience and the creative that produced them, the budget has somewhere obvious to move, and the conversation stops being about how many leads came in.

Paid social for ecommerce

Discovery, remarketing,
and sales.

For ecommerce businesses, paid social covers product discovery, remarketing, and sales campaigns. It is one of the few channels where somebody finds a product they were not looking for and buys it in the same session.

We structure campaigns around products, customer groups, previous website activity, and stages of the buying journey. A first-time visitor, someone who left a full cart, and a repeat customer are three audiences with three offers, and the store usually already knows which of them is worth the most.

We measure performance through product views, add-to-carts, purchases, and revenue. Reading the whole chain beats reading one conversion number at the end of it, because the chain shows where people are dropping out.

Measuring paid social performance

A campaign with plenty of impressions
can still be failing.

Impressions are the easiest number in the account to grow and the least useful one to manage against. They belong in the report as context, not as the headline, and a campaign can pile up plenty of them while the business gets nothing.

The most important metric depends on what the campaign is designed to achieve. A lead-generation campaign should not be judged in the same way as an ecommerce sales campaign, and reading either one against the wrong number is how a campaign that was working gets switched off.

Common paid social problems

The platform is usually
not the problem.

Paid social campaigns underperform for reasons that usually sit outside the advertising platform. Audiences left too broad, the same creative running for months, traffic sent to an unrelated landing page, messages never tested against each other, poor conversion tracking, retargeting audiences too small to serve, and clicks measured instead of actual business results.

None of those show up as an error in the ad account. They show up as a campaign that spends its budget on schedule and produces very little, which is the hardest kind of problem to notice from inside a monthly report.

Regular campaign reviews are what identify where changes are needed. Not every review ends in a change, but the ones that do usually start somewhere in that list rather than in the bidding.

FAQ

Straight answers
on paid social.

What is a paid social agency?+
A paid social agency manages advertising campaigns on social media platforms: audience targeting, campaign setup, creative testing, budget management, retargeting, and performance tracking.
Which platforms can businesses use for paid social advertising?+
The right platform depends on the target audience and the business objective. Facebook and Instagram are the common options, and other social platforms suit specific audiences.
Is paid social the same as PPC?+
Not exactly. PPC means paid advertising billed on clicks. Paid social means advertising through social media platforms. The two overlap, but they are not the same thing.
Is paid social good for local businesses?+
Yes, when the campaigns are targeted at the locations and audiences the business actually serves. The offer, the creative, the landing page, and the campaign objective matter just as much as the targeting.
How do you measure paid social campaigns?+
Against the campaign objective, whatever it was set to. The usual metrics are leads, purchases, cost per lead, cost per acquisition, conversion rate, revenue, and return on ad spend.
