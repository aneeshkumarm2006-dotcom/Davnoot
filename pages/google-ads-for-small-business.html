Google Ads for
small business.

Google Ads can give a small business a way to appear when potential customers are actively searching for its products or services. But with a limited advertising budget, every part of the campaign matters.

The goal isn't to get as many clicks as possible. It is to make sure the budget is reaching relevant searches, and that those visits have a clear path toward an enquiry, booking, phone call, or purchase.

Is it worth it

Google Ads works for
small businesses that pick a target.

It can work, but it depends on what you are selling, where you operate, how competitive your market is, and how much each new customer is worth to your business.

A local service business may use Google Ads to reach people searching for a service in a specific city. An ecommerce business may use it to promote particular products. A professional service business may focus on searches from people looking for a consultation.

The important part is choosing a campaign around a clear business objective rather than simply starting with a budget. If you sell online, the store-specific side of that work lives on our ecommerce agency page.

Start with the searches that matter

You cannot afford to advertise
for every search.

Keyword research should focus on searches that are closely connected to what you actually sell. More specific searches make it easier to understand what the person is looking for.

Someone searching for "plumber near me" has a very different intent from someone searching for "how to fix a leaking tap". The campaign should be built around the first kind.

Set a budget you can manage

There is no universal
small business ad budget.

Your budget depends on your industry, location, competition, average cost per click, and the value of a new customer. Three questions give you a better starting point than copying what another business spends.

It is also worth remembering that a small budget needs tighter targeting. If the campaign is showing for too many irrelevant searches, the money can disappear before the business gets enough useful data to learn anything from it.

01
Capacity

How much can we actually spend?

Not the number that sounds ambitious, but the number you can commit to for long enough to collect data. A budget that stops after three weeks buys you a partial answer at full price.

02
Definition

What counts as a valuable enquiry?

A phone call, a quote request, a booking, a purchase. Naming it precisely is what makes conversion tracking possible, and everything downstream depends on it.

03
Value

What is a new customer worth to us?

First sale, or the whole relationship. This is the number that decides whether a given cost per click is expensive or cheap, and it is different for every business.

How the account is built

A small account,
kept deliberately simple.

A small business doesn't need a complicated Google Ads account. It needs one that makes it obvious where the money is going and which parts are producing results.

01

Campaign structure

Start with the products or services that matter most. A home-service company might separate plumbing, emergency plumbing, and water heaters; an ecommerce business organizes around its important product categories.

02

Location targeting

A business serving Montreal may not want to spend money showing ads across Canada. Campaign settings should reflect the actual service area, and exclusions matter as much as inclusions.

03

Negative keywords

One of the easiest ways to waste a small budget is appearing for searches that aren't relevant. The Search Terms report shows what people actually typed, and that is where the exclusion list comes from.

04

Ad copy

Ads written for the specific search rather than for the business in general, with the next step stated plainly instead of left for the visitor to work out.

05

Landing pages

Recommendations for where each ad should send people, so the search, the ad, and the page all say the same thing and the enquiry is one action away.

06

Conversion tracking

Calls, forms, quote requests, bookings, and purchases tracked as separate actions, so the report compares advertising cost against results rather than against clicks.

Don't ignore negative keywords

The searches you
should never pay for.

A company selling a service usually does not want clicks from people looking for these. The Search Terms report reveals what people actually searched before clicking the advertisement, and reviewing it is what turns a guess into an exclusion list.

Track what happens after the click

A campaign can get hundreds of clicks
and produce very little business.

Getting the click is only the beginning. If someone searches for a specific service and the advertisement sends them to a generic homepage, they have to go looking for what they came for. The better path connects the search, the advertisement, and the landing page: search, relevant ad, relevant page, enquiry.

Once these actions are tracked, you can compare advertising cost against the results being generated instead of judging the campaign by impressions and clicks. Current small-business Google Ads guidance consistently emphasises relevant landing pages and conversion tracking for exactly this reason.

FAQ

Straight answers on
small business Google Ads.

How much should a small business spend on Google Ads?+
There is no fixed amount. The right budget depends on the industry, competition, location, cost per click, and the value of a new customer. Those four numbers are a better starting point than a figure borrowed from another business.
Are Google Ads good for small businesses with a small budget?+
They can be, provided the campaign is tightly targeted. A smaller budget makes it especially important to focus on relevant searches, locations, and services, because there is less room to absorb spend on clicks that were never going to convert.
What keywords should a small business use for Google Ads?+
Start with searches closely related to the products or services you sell. Location-based and service-specific searches are usually the most useful for a local business, because they tell you both what the person wants and whether you can serve them.
How can I avoid wasting money on Google Ads?+
Use focused keywords, appropriate location targeting, negative keywords, relevant landing pages, and conversion tracking. Review the Search Terms report regularly to identify irrelevant traffic before it has been paid for twice.
Should a small business send Google Ads traffic to its homepage?+
Not always. If the advertisement is for a specific product or service, a relevant landing page usually gives the visitor a clearer path to the information or action they are looking for than a homepage that has to introduce the whole business first.
How long before a small business campaign is worth judging?+
Long enough to collect a meaningful number of tracked conversions. Campaigns need enough data to evaluate, and constantly changing keywords, budgets, and settings makes it harder to understand what is actually working.
