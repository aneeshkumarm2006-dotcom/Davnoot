Why it works

Search engine marketing for
Montreal businesses.

A search is a short window. Someone types a query, scans the results, and picks. Search engine marketing puts a business inside that window, in front of people already looking for what it offers, which is why a search campaign produces enquiries in its first weeks while other channels are still assembling an audience.

Davnoot plans and manages SEM campaigns around relevant search terms, target locations, budgets, and measurable business goals. What the campaign has to achieve comes first. Keywords, ads, bids, and pages follow from it, not the other way round.

This page covers search engine marketing as a whole: strategy, keywords, ads, landing pages, tracking, and optimization. If you want the account-management side written up on its own, that lives on our PPC agency Montreal page, and the decision of how much of the budget search should get in the first place is covered on our media buying agency Montreal page.

What we manage

Six parts of a search campaign,
managed together.

A search campaign is a set of choices that only make sense against each other. We manage them as one piece of work rather than as a monthly checklist.

Each of the six below can be improved on its own and still leave the campaign where it started. Better keywords pointed at the wrong page, or a better page with nothing tracking what happens on it, both end the same way.

01

SEM campaign strategy

We agree on what the account has to produce before anything gets built, then separate campaigns by service, product, location, or search intent so each one can be read on its own instead of as an account average.

02

Keyword research and intent

We review commercial keywords, high-intent searches, the real search terms already triggering ads, keyword competition, negative keywords, and location-based searches, and keep the ones that relate closely to what the business sells.

03

Search ads and testing

Each advertisement has to match the search that brought someone to it. We test ad copy, headlines, offers, and calls to action against each other instead of writing one version and leaving it running for a year.

04

Landing pages that answer

Someone searching for a specific service should land on a page about that service, not a generic homepage. We check that the page behind each ad group gives the visitor a clear next step and one obvious way to take it.

05

Conversion tracking

Traffic earns its cost when it turns into a business result. We track contact forms, phone calls, quote requests, bookings, purchases, and lead forms as separate actions so it is clear which campaigns and keywords produce them.

06

Ongoing optimization

Search data keeps arriving after launch. We use it to adjust keywords, advertisements, bids, budgets, and landing pages over time, and we report on the numbers those decisions were made against.

Campaign strategy

A campaign is built around
what it has to produce.

A search campaign should be built around what the business wants to achieve. That sounds obvious, and it is still the step most often skipped. The account gets keywords before anyone has written down what a good month is supposed to look like.

A campaign gets pointed at one of these. Most accounts carry two or three at once, and we keep them in separate campaigns so one goal does not quietly absorb the budget meant for another.

We then split campaigns by service, product, location, or search intent. That split is what makes the reporting readable later. A service costing three times as much per enquiry is only visible if it was never mixed in with the rest.

Keyword research

Not every search is
equally valuable.

We research keywords based on what people are actually looking for and how closely that search relates to the business. Search volume is the least interesting number on the list, because a large audience asking the wrong question is still the wrong audience.

The review covers six things, and the last two usually save the most money. Negative keywords and location settings are what stop the budget from meeting searches the business has no way to serve.

This cuts irrelevant traffic and keeps the advertising budget on searches that can turn into work. In practice the keyword list gets shorter before the results get better.

Search ads

The ad should match
the search.

Every ad has to answer the query that surfaced it. When it does not, the click is paid for anyway and the visit is over in seconds, which is an expensive way to find out the wording was wrong.

We test different ad copy, headlines, offers, and calls to action rather than writing one version and treating it as settled. We compare ads inside the ad group they belong to, since a message that works for one service reads as noise against another.

Testing is only worth doing when something is counting the outcome. An ad that earns more clicks and fewer enquiries has still lost, and only the conversion data will say so.

Landing pages

The page has to finish
what the ad started.

Half of a search campaign happens after the click. We make sure the landing page offers a clear next step, and that the step is the one the campaign was built to produce rather than a general invitation to browse.

Someone searching for a specific service should land on a page that addresses that service, not a generic homepage. Asking a visitor to go hunting for the thing they just typed into a search box is where most of the drop-off happens.

Where a matching page does not exist yet, that is worth knowing before the budget goes live. It is usually cheaper to write the page than to keep paying for clicks that arrive somewhere close enough.

Local search in Montreal

SEM for businesses that
serve this city.

For a business serving Montreal, we structure campaigns around specific locations and service areas. The budget then reaches the customers the business actually serves instead of spreading across areas it cannot cover.

Where a business covers several areas, those areas are worth keeping in separate campaigns. One campaign covering the island, the South Shore, and everywhere in between tells you nothing about any of them, and the cheapest area quietly funds the most expensive one.

Campaigns can run in English, French, or both. In a bilingual market that is a targeting decision as much as a writing one, because the two audiences search with different words and rarely at the same cost.

Conversion tracking

Traffic only counts when
it does something.

A campaign counts when someone fills the form, picks up the phone, or asks for a quote. Until those actions are tracked, it can only be judged on how much attention it bought, and buying attention is the one thing every campaign is good at.

We count contact forms, phone calls, quote requests, bookings, purchases, and lead forms, each as its own action rather than one total. A booking and a newsletter signup are not the same event and should not carry the same weight.

That is how we see which campaigns and keywords produce meaningful results, and it is what lets us move budget on evidence instead of on the shape of a traffic graph.

Performance and optimization

A campaign can look busy
and still go nowhere.

These are the numbers we review campaign performance through. We read them as one set, on the cadence each one earns, rather than pulling them into a slide at the end of the month.

Nearly every change that improves one of these costs you another. A lower cost per click buys weaker traffic, and a higher conversion rate on a shrinking budget is not the win it looks like. A single metric is a poor reason to touch a campaign.

We use the data to adjust keywords, advertisements, bids, budgets, and landing pages over time. The aim is to get the same budget in front of more of the searches that lead somewhere, which usually means less traffic rather than more.

FAQ

Straight answers
on search engine marketing.

What does an SEM agency do?+
An SEM agency helps businesses use search engine marketing to reach relevant audiences through paid search campaigns, keyword targeting, advertisements, conversion tracking, and ongoing optimization. Those parts interact, so managing one without the others usually moves the problem rather than fixing it.
Is SEM the same as PPC?+
People use the terms interchangeably. SEM is the broader term for search engine marketing, while PPC refers specifically to pay-per-click advertising. In day-to-day work the label matters less than whether the campaign was built around a business goal.
Is SEM useful for local businesses?+
Yes. Search campaigns can be targeted at specific cities, regions, and service areas, which suits any business serving a defined local market. For a business with a fixed service radius, that targeting is often the largest saving available in the account.
How do you measure SEM results?+
We measure results through conversions, leads, sales, phone calls, cost per lead, cost per acquisition, revenue, and return on ad spend. Which of those leads the reporting depends on what the campaign was set up to produce.
